## Business Descriptions & Growth Drivers

**1. Rocket Lab (RKLB)** — Vertically integrated space company: the Electron small-launch
rocket plus a Space Systems arm (satellites, spacecraft components, Photon bus).
*Drivers:* debut and ramp of the reusable medium-lift **Neutron** rocket (a direct Falcon 9
challenger), growing national-security/defense launch awards, expansion into building and
operating satellites, and a rising launch + systems backlog.

**2. AST SpaceMobile (ASTS)** — Building a space-based cellular broadband network that connects
ordinary, unmodified smartphones directly to satellites (direct-to-device).
*Drivers:* deployment of its BlueBird constellation, carrier partnerships (AT&T, Verizon,
Vodafone), government/defense interest, and the transition from testing to paid commercial service.

**3. United Microelectronics (UMC)** — Taiwan-based pure-play semiconductor foundry focused on
mature and specialty process nodes.
*Drivers:* demand for specialty/analog/legacy chips, capacity expansion and geographic
diversification (Singapore, Japan, US partnerships), and pricing/utilization recovery.

**4. Astera Labs (ALAB)** — Connectivity silicon for AI and cloud data centers — PCIe/CXL/Ethernet
retimers and smart fabric switches (Aries, Taurus, Leo, Scorpio).
*Drivers:* the AI infrastructure buildout, surging need for high-speed links between GPUs, CPUs and
memory, scale-up fabric (Scorpio switches), and deep hyperscaler design-ins.

**5. Micron Technology (MU)** — Memory and storage maker: DRAM, NAND, and high-bandwidth memory (HBM).
*Drivers:* HBM demand for AI accelerators, a DRAM pricing up-cycle, data-center capacity growth, and
disciplined industry supply.

**6. Snowflake (SNOW)** — Cloud data platform for warehousing, analytics, and secure data sharing.
*Drivers:* AI/ML workloads (Cortex AI), consumption-based revenue growth, new-product adoption, and
enterprise data consolidation onto one platform.

**7. Hut 8 (HUT)** — Bitcoin miner and energy-infrastructure operator increasingly pivoting toward
high-performance computing.
*Drivers:* Bitcoin price, expanding owned power capacity, a shift into AI/HPC data-center hosting,
and a large strategic BTC treasury.

**8. Datadog (DDOG)** — Cloud observability and monitoring SaaS (infrastructure, APM, logs, security).
*Drivers:* ongoing cloud migration, module cross-sell, AI/LLM observability, and usage-based expansion
across large customers.

**9. Arm Holdings (ARM)** — Licenses CPU architecture and IP that powers most of the world's smartphones
and a growing share of data-center and edge chips.
*Drivers:* Armv9 royalty-rate uplift, data-center CPU penetration (Neoverse, Nvidia Grace), AI-edge and
automotive growth, and a move toward selling fuller compute subsystems.

**10. Sterling Infrastructure (STRL)** — E-infrastructure and construction services: data-center site
development, transportation, and building solutions.
*Drivers:* the data-center construction boom, manufacturing reshoring, federal infrastructure spending,
and a mix shift toward higher-margin e-infrastructure work.

**11. Flex (FLEX)** — Global contract electronics manufacturer (EMS) and design partner across multiple
end markets.
*Drivers:* AI data-center hardware (power, racks, liquid cooling via JetCool), reshoring of manufacturing,
embedded power, and automotive/health programs.

**12. Semtech (SMTC)** — Analog and mixed-signal semiconductors, including LoRa IoT connectivity and
data-center signal-integrity products.
*Drivers:* AI data-center active-copper interconnect demand (CopperEdge), LoRa IoT expansion, and recovery
across cyclical end markets.

**13. Super Micro Computer (SMCI)** — Designs and builds AI-optimized servers, storage, and rack-scale
systems with leading liquid-cooling capability.
*Drivers:* GPU/AI server demand (close Nvidia alignment), direct-liquid-cooling leadership, and large
data-center buildouts — partly offset by margin and governance scrutiny.

**14. Guardant Health (GH)** — Precision-oncology company specializing in blood-based (liquid biopsy)
cancer tests.
*Drivers:* adoption and reimbursement of the **Shield** colorectal-cancer screening test, an expanding
test menu, minimal-residual-disease (MRD) testing, and rising clinical volumes.

**15. AAON (AAON)** — Manufacturer of semi-custom HVAC equipment, including high-efficiency rooftop units
and data-center cooling.
*Drivers:* data-center liquid-cooling and thermal-management demand (BasX), a strong order backlog, and
energy-efficiency-driven equipment replacement.

**16. IonQ (IONQ)** — Quantum-computing company using trapped-ion technology, delivered via the cloud and
on-prem systems.
*Drivers:* qubit-scaling roadmap, cloud availability (AWS, Azure, Google), quantum-networking acquisitions,
and government/enterprise contracts.

**17. Dell Technologies (DELL)** — PCs, servers, storage, and infrastructure solutions.
*Drivers:* a fast-growing AI-server backlog (ISG), the AI-PC refresh cycle, enterprise storage, and
operating leverage as AI infrastructure scales.

**18. Fortinet (FTNT)** — Cybersecurity vendor known for firewalls, secure networking, SASE, and security
operations.
*Drivers:* the firewall refresh cycle, SASE and cloud-security expansion, AI-driven SecOps, and unified
platform adoption.

**19. Riot Platforms (RIOT)** — Large US Bitcoin miner with low-cost Texas power and data-center ambitions.
*Drivers:* Bitcoin price, low-cost/curtailable power, hash-rate growth, and a pivot toward AI/HPC hosting.

**20. Applied Digital (APLD)** — Builds and operates data centers for HPC/AI workloads and GPU cloud services.
*Drivers:* large AI/HPC capacity leases to hyperscalers and neoclouds, a sizeable power-development pipeline,
and growth in its cloud GPU business.

**21. DigitalOcean (DOCN)** — Cloud platform serving developers, startups, and SMBs.
*Drivers:* AI/ML offerings (GPU droplets, Paperspace), growth in higher-spend customers, and broadening of
its managed-services and AI product set.

**22. CrowdStrike (CRWD)** — Cloud-native cybersecurity built on the Falcon platform (endpoint, identity,
cloud, SIEM).
*Drivers:* platform consolidation and module cross-sell, Next-Gen SIEM, cloud and identity security, the
Charlotte AI assistant, and strong recurring-revenue growth.

**23. Cipher Mining (CIFR)** — Bitcoin miner expanding into HPC/AI data centers.
*Drivers:* Bitcoin price, build-out of low-cost power capacity, and conversion of sites toward AI
data-center hosting agreements.

**24. D-Wave Quantum (QBTS)** — Quantum-computing company offering annealing and gate-model systems aimed at
optimization problems.
*Drivers:* commercial optimization use-cases, its Advantage2 system, growing bookings, and
government/commercial contracts.

**25. Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)** — CPUs and GPUs across data center, client, gaming, and embedded markets.
*Drivers:* the MI300/MI350/MI400 AI-accelerator roadmap challenging Nvidia, EPYC server-share gains, AI-PC
adoption, and data-center momentum.

**26. First Solar (FSLR)** — Largest US solar-module maker, using differentiated thin-film (CdTe) technology.
*Drivers:* US manufacturing tax credits (IRA Section 45X), domestic-content demand, a multi-year contracted
backlog, and surging power demand from data centers.

**27. Palo Alto Networks (PANW)** — Broad cybersecurity platform spanning network security, cloud (Prisma),
and security operations (Cortex).
*Drivers:* its "platformization" strategy, AI-powered security, SASE growth, and rising next-gen-security ARR.

**28. IREN Limited (IREN)** — Renewable-powered operator running Bitcoin mining and AI cloud/data-center services.
*Drivers:* Bitcoin price, expansion of GPU/AI cloud capacity, low-cost renewable power, and data-center buildout.

**29. Ford Motor (F)** — Global automaker spanning trucks/ICE (Ford Blue), EVs (Model e), and commercial
(Ford Pro).
*Drivers:* the high-margin Ford Pro commercial + software franchise, strong hybrid demand, cost reduction in EVs,
and the F-Series truck franchise — with EV losses still a headwind. (A ~40% one-month move is large for a stock
this size, suggesting a specific earnings or strategic catalyst.)

**30. Planet Labs (PL)** — Operates one of the largest Earth-observation satellite fleets and sells imagery plus
AI-driven geospatial analytics.
*Drivers:* expanding defense/government contracts, large multi-year data deals, and value-added AI analytics
(Planetary Variables) layered on top of raw imagery.

## Thematic Takeaways

The leaderboard is dominated by a few clear themes:
- **AI infrastructure / semiconductors:** MU, ALAB, ARM, AMD, SMCI, DELL, UMC, SMTC, FLEX, AAON, STRL.
- **Crypto-miners pivoting to AI/HPC data centers:** HUT, RIOT, CIFR, IREN, APLD.
- **Space & satellites:** RKLB, ASTS, PL.
- **Cybersecurity & cloud software:** CRWD, PANW, FTNT, DDOG, SNOW, DOCN.
- **Quantum computing:** IONQ, QBTS.
- **Other catalysts:** GH (Shield screening), FSLR (US solar policy), F (auto catalyst).
